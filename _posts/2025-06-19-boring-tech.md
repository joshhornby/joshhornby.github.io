It's 2025 and this isn't new advice. But some reminders are worth repeating: choose boring tech if you're in the business of product engineering.

It's easy to get pulled into the latest frameworks and the not-quite-stable libraries. The real edge, especially for a small team, is putting all your focus into the product rather than the plumbing. Boring tech isn't about being cautious. It's about being deliberate. The stack should fade into the background so you can keep your hands on the work that matters.

To borrow from [Trainspotting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainspotting_(film)): choose life. Or, at least, choose a life where your stack isn't the most interesting thing about your company. Choose boring frameworks. Choose reliable databases. Choose proven languages. Choose not to spend your weekends deciphering cryptic errors in a bleeding-edge runtime. Choose to ship. Customers remember what you build, not what you built it with.

Every hour you save by not reinventing the wheel is an hour you spend moving the product forward. That's the whole argument.
